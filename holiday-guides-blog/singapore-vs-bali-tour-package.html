Singapore Tour Package vs Bali Tour Package: Finding the Best Singapore Tour Package from India

HolidayGuides Editors
August 1, 2026 7 min read
Article Navigation

    Choosing between Singapore and Bali for a Southeast Asian vacation usually comes down to what you want out of your daily schedule. Do you prefer smooth transit systems, skyline views, and theme parks, or are you looking for tropical beaches, relaxed beach clubs, and quiet rice terraces? Both destinations are within a direct flight range of roughly five to six hours from major Indian metros like Delhi, Mumbai, and Bengaluru, but they deliver completely different experiences.

    If you are evaluating the best Singapore tour package from India against an island getaway in Bali, understanding the key differences in daily costs, local travel, logistics, and sightseeing pace will help you pick the right fit.

    Daily Budget and Flight Expenses

    Budgeting for these two destinations requires looking beyond the initial flight ticket. Direct flights to both Singapore and Denpasar (Bali) generally range between ₹20,000 and ₹35,000 round-trip depending on how early you book and whether you travel during peak season. However, daily ground expenses differ significantly.

    Singapore operates on a strong currency (1 SGD is roughly ₹60-₹62). A comfortable mid-range hotel usually starts around ₹10,000 to ₹15,000 per night. While dining at local hawker centers keeps food bills reasonable at ₹500 to ₹800 per meal, paid entry fees for high-end attractions like Universal Studios, Night Safari, and Gardens by the Bay can add up quickly. A structured 5-day Singapore itinerary typically costs between ₹75,000 and ₹1,10,000 per person, excluding flights.

    Bali offers lower day-to-day land costs. You can rent a private pool villa in Ubud or Seminyak for ₹6,000 to ₹10,000 per night. Local meals, private car rentals with a driver, and café visits are relatively light on the wallet. However, because sights in Bali are spread across the island, local transport costs can increase if you move between regions like Canggu, Uluwatu, and Sidemen every few days.

    Transportation and Moving Around

    Getting around Singapore is straightforward. The MRT (Mass Rapid Transit) subway network reaches every corner of the city, including direct access from Changi Airport. An EZ-Link transit card or a simple tap of an Indian contactless credit card lets you ride buses and trains without needing taxis. Everything runs strictly on schedule, and walking between neighborhoods like Chinatown, Kampong Glam, and Marina Bay is simple thanks to air-conditioned walkways and clear signage.

    Bali has no public rail system. Travelers rely entirely on private drivers, taxis, or scooters. While hiring a car with a driver for ten hours costs around ₹2,500 to ₹3,500, traffic in tourist heavy areas like Kuta, Seminyak, and Ubud can be dense during peak hours. A ten-kilometer drive can easily take over an hour. If you prefer fixed schedules and swift transit, Singapore holds a clear advantage. If you do not mind sitting back in an air-conditioned car while taking in the views, Bali fits fine.

    Visa Requirements for Indian Passport Holders

    Logistics before departure are straightforward for both destinations, though processing details differ:

    • Singapore Tourist Visa: Indian travelers need a tourist visa prior to departure. The visa is processed electronically through authorized agents, costs around ₹2,100 plus agent processing fees, and typically takes 3 to 5 working days. Multiple-entry visas with validities ranging from 9 weeks to 2 years are common.
    • Bali (Indonesia) Visa on Arrival: Indian passport holders can obtain a Visa on Arrival (VoA) at Denpasar airport or apply online beforehand for an e-VoA. The cost is IDR 500,000 (roughly ₹2,700) for a 30-day stay, which can be extended once while in Indonesia.

    Food Preferences and Vegetarian Dining

    Indian travelers often worry about food availability abroad. Both destinations handle dietary requirements well, but in distinct ways.

    Singapore has a massive South Indian and North Indian diaspora. The Little India precinct offers hundreds of authentic restaurants serving everything from pure vegetarian thalis to Chettinad specialties. Additionally, major shopping malls and hawker centers maintain dedicated stalls for vegetarian, halal, and Jain diets.

    Bali is deeply rooted in Balinese Hindu culture, which means vegetarian and vegan foods are widespread, particularly around Ubud. Smoothies, avocado toasts, plant-based cafes, and traditional dishes like Gado-Gado (vegetable salad with peanut sauce) are available almost everywhere. Finding pure vegetarian or Indian food in Bali requires little effort, though non-vegetarian dishes heavily feature pork and seafood in local eateries (warungs).

    Sightseeing Pace and Attractions

    The choice often comes down to what you want to see when you wake up in the morning.

    Singapore Pace VS Bali Pace
    High-energy, organized city vibe ⚡ Relaxed, scenic, nature-centered
    Walking-heavy & MRT subway travel 🚇 Private driver days & scenic drives
    Futuristic skylines & supertrees 🏙️ Beaches, cliff views & waterfalls
    Theme parks & night light shows 🎡 Ancient temples & lush rice terraces

    Singapore is organized, modern, and energetic. A typical day involves walking through the massive glass bio-domes at Gardens by the Bay, spending a full day on Sentosa Island for theme park rides, and watching the evening light and water show at Marina Bay. It is a fantastic option for families traveling with children or seniors who appreciate clean infrastructure and minimal road travel friction.

    Bali is about natural landscapes, wellness, and beach days. Its itinerary features early morning sunrise views at Mount Batur, visiting cliffside temples like Uluwatu, exploring waterfalls near Munduk, and relaxing at beach clubs. The pace is generally slower, making it a favorite for couples, honeymoons, and solo travelers looking to unwind rather than tick off urban attractions.

    Common Planning Mistakes to Avoid

    1. Overpacking the Singapore Itinerary: Many first-time visitors try to visit Sentosa, the Singapore Zoo, Orchard Road, and Marina Bay all in two days. You will end up walking 15,000 steps a day in high humidity. Allow 4 to 5 days to cover the city comfortably without exhaustion.
    2. Underestimating Bali Distances: Booking a hotel in Seminyak and planning daily day trips to Lempuyang Temple (Gates of Heaven) or Nusa Penida requires hours of travel time. Split your stay between two regions—such as 3 nights in Ubud for culture and nature, and 3 nights in Seminyak or Sanur for the coast.
    3. Ignoring Seasonal Weather: Singapore is warm and humid year-round, with brief rain showers occurring almost daily. Bali has a distinct dry season (April to October) and a wet season (November to March). If you plan a beach holiday during the Indian winter months, expect occasional heavy monsoonal downpours in Bali.
    HolidayGuides Verdict: Pick Singapore if you want a crisp 4-5 day futuristic city holiday. Pick Bali if you want a 7-10 day tropical escape with private pool villas and beach sunsets!

    Final Verdict: Which Fits Your Travel Style?

    Pick Singapore if you want a smooth, futuristic city break with world-class attractions, easy public transport, and endless family entertainment options. It is ideal for a crisp, 4 to 5-day holiday where everything runs like clockwork.

    Pick Bali if you want an open-ended tropical vacation centered on nature, private pool villas, scenic cafe cultures, and coastal sunsets. If you have 7 to 10 days to spare and prefer a slower rhythm, Bali delivers great value for money.
